How to Spot a Phishing Link

  • Look at the actual domain: Check the part right before the first slash (e.g., paypal.com.fake.xyz is fake.xyz, NOT paypal).
  • Beware of raw IP addresses: Legitimate banking or shopping services almost never ask you to open a raw numerical IP address like http://185.22.x.x.
  • Excessive hyphens: Look out for domains like apple-support-login-verify.com designed to confuse you.

5 Red Flags in Scam Messages

  • Artificial Urgency: Demands like "Act within 24 hours or your account will be deleted" try to stop you from thinking clearly.
  • Requests for OTPs or Passwords: Legitimate companies will never ask you to text or email your one-time passcode.
  • Unsolicited Prize / Lottery: You cannot win a lottery you never entered.
  • Threats of Police or Arrest: Law enforcement never contacts citizens via SMS to demand payments.

Why SSL & Encryption Matter

  • Prevents Eavesdropping: HTTPS encrypts communications so hackers on public Wi-Fi cannot see passwords or card numbers.
  • Identity Verification: Legitimate SSL certificates are verified by trusted Certificate Authorities (CAs).
  • However: Having a padlock does NOT mean a website is 100% honest — scammers can also obtain free certificates. Always verify the domain name itself.

File Hash Fingerprinting

  • What is a hash? A cryptographic hash (like SHA-256) is a unique digital fingerprint of a file.
  • Integrity Check: If even one single character in a file changes, the entire hash changes completely.
  • Malware Detection: Antivirus and threat intelligence databases maintain hashes of known viruses and ransomware to block them instantly.
